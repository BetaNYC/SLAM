Places and district lines

Community board / community district
New York City has 59 community districts. Each has a community board of up to 50 unpaid members appointed by the Borough President, at least half of them nominated by the district's Council members. Those Council members also sit on the board without a vote. Boards give advice on liquor licenses, land use and City services in their district. SLAM shows one district at a time.
District number
SLAM numbers districts the way the Department of City Planning does. The first digit is the borough (1 Manhattan, 2 Bronx, 3 Brooklyn, 4 Queens, 5 Staten Island) and the last two are the district. So 101 is Manhattan Community District 1 and 304 is Brooklyn Community District 4.
Placed in a district
SLAM takes each record's map point and checks which community district line it falls inside. This is called a point-in-polygon check: "which shape is this dot inside?" The lines come from City Planning's Community Districts dataset.
Address page
One page for each address that has a record in SLAM. Records for the same building share a page when the City's address file matches them to the same BIN. Otherwise a page is keyed on the street address and ZIP code.
The City's address file (PAD)
The City Planning Department's Property Address Directory: every address in the city with its building (BIN), tax lot (BBL) and map point. SLAM matches addresses against the copy NYC Planning Labs prepares for its GeoSearch service. A match needs the same house number, street and borough. SLAM also tries common ways addresses are written, such as a Queens number without its hyphen ("104 30" for "104-30") or a range ("237-243"); readings that could land on the wrong building must be close to the record's own map point.
GeoSearch
The City's address search service, built from the same address file. SLAM asks it only about addresses the file cannot match, and the address box at the top of the site uses it. A match counts only when the house number, street and borough are the same, and GeoSearch's confidence is 0.8 or higher.
Low-confidence match
GeoSearch answered, but no result had the same house number, street and borough with a confidence of 0.8 or higher. The address page stays keyed on the street address and ZIP code.
BIN (Building Identification Number)
A number the City gives each building. SLAM uses it to put records for the same building on one address page.
BBL (borough, block and lot)
The City's number for a tax lot, the piece of land a building stands on. One lot can hold several buildings.

Licenses and permits

SLA (State Liquor Authority)
The New York State agency that issues and enforces liquor licenses. Its Board holds Full Board meetings about every three weeks to decide licensing and disciplinary matters.
LAMP
SLA's public map of licenses and applications (lamp.sla.ny.gov). SLAM links to it so you can see SLA's own record for a place.
Pending application
A liquor license application SLA has received and not yet decided.
Active / inactive license
An active license is in effect now. An inactive license is no longer in effect. SLA's inactive list gives the status for each one.
On-premises / off-premises
An on-premises license is for drinks served and drunk where they are sold, like a bar or restaurant. An off-premises license is for sealed bottles to take away, like a liquor or grocery store. The 200- and 500-foot rules in ABC Law §64 are about on-premises liquor licenses.
License type and class
SLA's codes for each kind of license. SLAM shows the code and SLA's own description. SLA publishes the full list as an Excel file (linked on the About page).
OCM (Office of Cannabis Management)
The New York State agency that licenses cannabis businesses, including adult-use retail dispensaries.
Dining Out NYC
The City's outdoor dining program, run by the Department of Transportation (DOT). It licenses sidewalk seating and roadway seating, year-round or seasonal. It took the place of the temporary, pandemic-era Open Restaurants program.
Permitted street event
A street fair, block party or similar event with a City permit. The source has no map points, so these appear in the list only.

311 complaints

311 service request (complaint)
A report someone made to 311. It is not a finding that anything happened. One problem can lead to several reports if several people report it.
Complaint type and descriptor
The category 311 gives a report, with a more specific descriptor. For example, complaint type "Noise - Commercial" with descriptor "Loud Music/Party," or "Drinking" with the descriptor "Underage - Licensed Est" (underage drinking at a licensed place).
Location type
Where 311 says the problem is, chosen when the report is made. SLAM keeps reports with location type "Club/Bar/Restaurant" or "Restaurant/Bar/Deli/Bakery", plus every "Outdoor Dining" complaint, whose location type is the sidewalk or street. The first SLAM used the same location filter.
Noise - Commercial
The 311 complaint type for noise from a business, such as loud music or a crowd. NYPD handles it.
Drinking
The 311 complaint type for drinking in public, or a licensed place serving after hours or to people under 21. NYPD handles it.
Outdoor Dining (311)
The 311 complaint type about outdoor dining setups, such as a blocked sidewalk or a seating area set up without approval. DOT handles it.
Map colors for 311
The map colors 311 dots in four groups, based on the first SLAM's layers: Noise - Commercial, Drinking, Outdoor Dining (a separate layer in the first SLAM), and Other (every other complaint type).

Restaurants and buildings

Health inspection
The Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH) inspects restaurants. It gives each problem it finds a number of points. Fewer points is better.
Letter grade
  • A: 0 to 13 points.
  • B: 14 to 27 points.
  • C: 28 or more points.
  • Grade pending: the restaurant may contest the result, so no letter is final yet.
  • Grade pending (re-opening): a grade pending after the restaurant re-opened.
  • Not yet graded: the restaurant has not been graded yet.
Certificate of occupancy (CO)
A Department of Buildings (DOB) document that records a building's use and how many people may occupy it. Newer COs are in DOB NOW. Older ones are in DOB's Buildings Information System (BIS).
PLUTO and building class
PLUTO is the City Planning dataset of every tax lot. Its building class codes M1 to M9 mark religious property.
Facilities Database (FacDB)
City Planning's list of public and private facilities. SLAM takes its K-12 schools.

Schools, houses of worship and distance rules

School
A K-12 school in the Facilities Database: public, charter, non-public and special education schools. Day care, pre-K, college and after-school programs are not included.
House of worship
A tax lot in PLUTO building class M1 (church, synagogue, chapel).
Other religious property
A tax lot in PLUTO building class M2, M3, M4 or M9 (mission house, parsonage or rectory, convent, miscellaneous). PLUTO does not say it is a place of worship.
200-foot rule
State law (ABC Law §64(7)(a)) about on-premises liquor licenses on the same street or avenue as, and within 200 feet of, a building used only as a school or place of worship. See the rules page.
500-foot rule
State law (ABC Law §64(7)(b)) about on-premises liquor licenses within 500 feet of three or more existing on-premises licensed premises. SLA may still grant one after a hearing if, after consulting the community board, it finds the license is in the public interest (§64(7)(f)). See the rules page.
Cannabis: 500-foot school rule
State law (Cannabis Law §72(6)(a)) about adult-use cannabis dispensaries on the same street as, and within 500 feet of, a building containing a school. It is a different law from the liquor rules: 500 feet for schools, not 200, and the building only has to contain a school. Read §72.
Cannabis: 200-foot house of worship rule
State law (Cannabis Law §72(6)(b)) about adult-use cannabis dispensaries on the same street as, and within 200 feet of, a building used only as a house of worship. Like the liquor rules, it is measured from entrance to entrance (§72(6)(c)). A renewal is not denied because of these two rules (§72(6)(f)). Address pages with a cannabis record list schools within about 500 ft and houses of worship within about 200 ft, building point to building point.
Community board notice (cannabis and liquor)
In New York City, an applicant must notify the community board before applying: for a liquor license, under ABC Law §110-b; for a cannabis dispensary or on-site consumption license, 30 to 270 days ahead, under Cannabis Law §76. The board may send its opinion for or against, and the opinion becomes part of the record. For cannabis, the Cannabis Control Board must answer the community board in writing, explaining how its opinion was considered.
Who decides
For liquor, the State Liquor Authority decides each application, including how these rules apply to it. For cannabis, the Office of Cannabis Management makes a recommendation and the Cannabis Control Board decides. In both cases the community board's opinion becomes part of the record (see Community board notice). SLAM lists what is nearby and how far away it is. It does not make that decision.

How the numbers are counted

Building point
SLAM puts each address page at one map point. When the City's address file matches the address, it is the City's point for that address. Otherwise it is the average of the records' own points.
Approximate distance
SLAM cannot measure door to door, because no dataset it uses has entrance locations. It measures straight lines from building point to building point. The liquor and cannabis laws measure from the center of the nearest entrance to the center of the nearest entrance, so every distance in SLAM is approximate. Building points can sit away from the door, so a SLAM distance can differ by tens of feet or more, especially for large or corner buildings. A distance near a limit is close, not over or under it.
311 at this address
A complaint counts at an address when its incident address and ZIP code, cleaned up the same way as the page's address, match the page. A complaint with only an intersection is never counted at an address.
311 nearby
Complaints whose map point is within 150 meters (about 490 feet) of the address page's point, not counting the ones at the address. They are shown as counts by complaint type.
Nearby addresses
Other address pages within 150 meters of this one, in the same district's data.
Within about 200 feet
The schools, houses of worship and other religious property whose point is within about 200 feet of the address page's point.
Same street
SLAM compares the street name in the school's or lot's address with the street names in the address page's records, after removing house numbers and floor or suite text. It shows one of three answers:
  • Same street name: the names match.
  • Different street name in the records: the names differ. A corner building fronts two streets, but the records list one address each, so this is not a "no."
  • Not known: one side has no usable street name.
Spelling differences, like "Saint Nicolas" and "St Nicholas," can still be missed.
On-premises licenses within about 500 feet
The number of other addresses with an active on-premises liquor license (SLA type 1, classes 0340 to 0421) within about 500 feet of the address page's point. Licenses at the address itself, pending applications and inactive licenses are not counted. Licenses in Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens are counted, because the Brooklyn pilot district borders Queens.
Premises (for counting)
One address page, or one street address and ZIP code for a license with no page. Several licenses at one address count as one premises.

Dates and how fresh the data is

Data updated
When SLAM last downloaded the data: Nov 5, 2026, 2:22 PM. It is shown at the top of every page.
Rows updated
When each dataset was last changed at its source, as the portal reports it. It is listed for every dataset on the About page.
311 date range
SLAM keeps 311 complaints created on or after 2022-01-01. The latest in this build is from 2026-11-04. Complaints from 2010 to 2019 are in a separate City dataset that SLAM does not read.
Today's district lines
Every record is placed using today's community district lines, including old records.
